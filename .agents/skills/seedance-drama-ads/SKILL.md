# Seedance Drama Ads

Create beautiful, original stories around any product.
Remain an independent prompt-only leaf. Never generate, acquire, render,
publish or modify production state. The caller owns production and approvals.
Entertainment-first describes emphasis, not a license to miss a locked reveal
deadline or a claim that stories outperform other ad formats.

## Creative freedom

Let the product, the brief and the story inspire the approach. Do not prescribe
conflict, dialogue, physical action, twists or a product-solving payoff as
defaults. Choose the form, tone, pace, sound and ending that suit the idea;
quiet, lively, lyrical, comic, emotional and other approaches are all available.
These are possibilities, not a required menu or a new formula.

A product may have a functional, emotional, symbolic or incidental role.
It need not solve a problem or drive every beat. Keep factual implications
truthful without limiting fictional imagination to demonstrated product uses.
Follow explicit creative choices and existing locks, not patterns from examples
or earlier agent-authored concepts. Do not require a framework questionnaire
for an open brief. Judge the story as a whole against the user's intent rather
than counting lines, actions, turns or cuts.

## Scope and inputs

Use this capability for original short stories around products, across genres
and visual treatments. No particular conflict, performance intensity, narrative
structure or soundtrack is required.
Understated comedy built around an observing documentary camera has a
different owner: deadpan ads. Real customer accounts belong to testimonial
work. A sequence explaining product features without a story is a demo.

Separate story form from treatment. Talking objects can be fictional characters;
cinematic live action, 3D cartoon, crochet and felt can carry the same story.
None supplies a plot or product claim by itself. A pure jingle belongs to
musical ads; a beauty montage belongs to cinematic B-roll. For a story-led
hybrid, keep this skill as story owner and describe only the requested style
or sound layer. Do not invoke siblings or copy another brand's characters.

Read supplied material and identify:

- Audience reaction, genre/register, one message and intended next action.
- Product/service identity, selected references, supported facts and limits.
- Existing story canon and any supplied character, plot or ending choices.
- Product role, reveal deadline, brand constraints and exact approved copy.
- Runtime, ratio, placement, generation count, native-cut/continuous-take locks.
- Cast/likeness/voice rights, environment, language and sound route.
- Permitted fiction, forbidden implications and any actual reference footage.
- Revision scope, review_mode, approval_mode and existing selections.

When foundations are missing, ask only what changes the story materially.
Offer an explicitly hypothetical premise for exploration, not invented canon
or a generation-ready package with fabricated references. Do not infer market,
culture, genre or cast from the operator. Do not invent testimonials or offers.
Reference-derived shot claims require inspected footage; articles and titles
alone do not establish an ad's staging or cut times.

Read [evaluation cases](references/evaluation-cases.md) when evaluating or
changing this skill or diagnosing weak dialogue, action or development.
Read [format notes](references/format-notes.md) for the public format source
and its evidence limits when discussing the reference or revising this guidance.

## Shape a compact drama brief

Accept a product image, supplied reference or website URL as the starting
pointer, not proof of product capabilities. Consume the caller's sourced
product facts and brand kit when available. This leaf does not browse,
download or create those assets. If the page is unreadable or facts absent,
ask for the material needed for the story; keep any exploratory premise
explicitly provisional. Never claim to have inspected inaccessible content.

Summarize the supplied logo asset, colors, typography, tone, supported selling
point and prohibited implications before expanding a new full concept. Unknown
values stay unknown; a photograph is not a verified palette or brand kit.
Keep existing approved values unchanged and surface only actual conflicts or
consequential gaps for confirmation, not a new mandatory approval ceremony.

For an open short-form brief, propose a 9:16 story under a minute for the
selected social placement. These are editable format suggestions, not platform
specifications or Seedance per-source limits. Explicit duration, ratio and
placement always win. Choose a story that fits the verified source scope.
Pitch two compact, materially different premises only when direction is open;
return one when settled or follow the requested count. Do not expand full
source prompts before the concept is selected.

Keep the concept handoff compact, reusing supplied brief fields. Include only
applicable creative fields rather than filling a mandatory story template:

```text
product and sourced selling point / audience reaction / next action
brand anchors and unknowns / fiction boundary / selected story form
premise / cast roles and relationships / visual treatment / audio route
story progression / character moments / ending
dialogue, action, music or silence as appropriate
product role / first product-identifiable moment / any requested reveal
runtime and ratio / ad count / source count / exact post CTA and destination
```

Prefer the smallest cast that makes the relationship readable; preserve
requested ensembles. Give each role a want, knowledge and observable response,
not just demographic labels. Proposed fictional casting is not an approved
identity reference, real-person consent or a testimonial.

## Optional hook, twist and reveal

Use this pattern only when selected for the concept, not as the definition of
a short drama. For that form, make these jobs distinct:

| Beat | Visible job | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Introduce the story's invitation or opening question | Unsupported shock claim or unrelated clickbait |
| Development | Develop the selected story toward its turn | Beats that do not serve the selected idea |
| Twist | Planted evidence lets an action or intention acquire a different meaning | Unseen rescuer, arbitrary betrayal or a product suddenly gaining a power |
| Reveal/payoff | Resolve the opening question through a supported product-linked action | Unrelated packshot, magical cure or a cliffhanger with no ad payoff |
| Next action | Leave readable space for the approved CTA and product recognition | Rushed end card, invented offer or dialogue explaining the entire twist |

A product can be identifiable early while its role becomes clear later.
Record those two moments separately; a late story reveal never overrides an
early product-reveal deadline. Hook, twist and reveal are narrative jobs, not
three mandatory shots or extra generations. Preserve an approved no-turn or
observational story instead of retrofitting this pattern.

Plan timing and sound around the selected story and placement. Where sound-off
viewing matters, consider captions in post without replacing sound-led
storytelling. Give the chosen moments and any requested CTA room to read.
Planning does not establish actual comprehension or audience response.

## 1. Establish the causal engine

Apply the bespoke five-element engine below only when selected. It is an
option, not a universal story or production gate. Preserve supplied
observational, episodic, non-causal and no-turn forms under their selected
criteria; ask for missing established context when continuing an existing
story. Engine definitions and verdict ledgers below apply only when selected.

When using this engine, test its causal links through behavior rather than
merely filling five fields. A failed attempt changes the next action under
this framework; do not apply that requirement to other story forms.
Technical identity and rights ledgers stay in the handoff; their essential
visible constraints remain in the prompt.

Use these bespoke dramatic definitions consistently, not the textbook meanings:

- **Goal:** the single, unchanging thing the hero fights for, fixing something
  already established and reaching the resolving result as fast as possible.
  The hero is never wrong about the major goal; every scene goal must be a
  causal link toward it. Remove a scene to test whether the chain still holds.
- **Obstacle:** a strong circumstance that jeopardizes a stage or the whole
  goal, not merely a cost. Name exactly what is at risk and local/global scale.
  Obstacle plus stakes makes the necessary move non-optional.
- **Tactic:** a reasonable guess forced by that threat on current, incomplete
  knowledge. The hero may misread circumstances, not be arbitrarily foolish.
  Each failed tactic returns information and reshapes the next move; failure
  without learning is wheel-spinning.
- **Reversal:** a turn against expectation, through one's action flipping
  against its intention, hidden agency being revealed, or the situation
  turning opposite to the tactic's intention.
- **Value Shift:** the audience's changed verdict on a character, triggered
  by a reversal, not the character's mood or a product becoming more valuable.
  Name before-verdict and after-verdict. A turn without this reappraisal is
  inert, not a functioning reversal.

A sequence opens when a specific jeopardy threatens a stage/sub-goal and ends
when it resolves or defeats the hero into a new path. Each resolved sequence,
including nested resolving units, needs a reversal with an audience reappraisal.
A single scene can be pure escalation; an unfinished excerpt is not a failed
resolved sequence. Build a deepening verdict trajectory, not random flip-flops.
Unknown story context limits the judgment; do not invent missing evidence.

## 2. Find the product's place in the story

For the selected causal engine, write `established trouble -> reasonable
attempt -> new information -> changed action -> payoff and audience reappraisal`.
For another selected form, explain the product's role under that form's
criteria without inventing a failed tactic or character reappraisal.
Let the product's place emerge naturally from the idea. A functional use,
personal association, background presence or imagined world can all fit.
Do not force the product to be an indispensable tool, a solution or a reveal.
Fictional events must not imply unsupported health, efficacy or financial claims.
Product visibility may be early or delayed according to the actual brief.

Map every factual implication to `claim_id / source / wording / conditions /
status / story beat`. Include visual results and comparisons, not only speech.
Keep fictional events distinguishable from factual capability. A whimsical
world can contain fiction without suggesting unproved efficacy.
Generated before/after scenes are not proof; jokes and disclaimers do not
substantiate claims. Keep unsupported numbers, personal histories and awards out.

## 3. Record knowledge, behavior and staging

Use this knowledge ledger only for a selected causal framework:

```text
sequence_id / jeopardy opens and resolves / fixed goal / stakes
beat_id / established evidence or proposed fiction / obstacle and scale
character knowledge before / tactic / returned information / next move
reversal form / audience verdict before -> after / supporting behavior
product role / claim_ids / reveal state / continuity in and out
```

Mark intended audience verdicts as interpretations, not observed audience
research. Ground them in the supplied story when that framework applies.
Track who can see or hear the new information and when.

Translate each beat into shootable coverage:

```text
shot_id / source_id / approximate window / story purpose
featured character / position / eyeline / prop possession / state_in
visible action / partner response / state_out / knowledge change
size / angle / lens intent / camera path and reason
named light source / key side / quality / motivated changes
reference roles / dialogue / audio owner / transition and handles
```

Use contrast where it clarifies the story: wide for geography, closer for a
decisive action or reappraisal. Do not demand a close-up on every beat.
Carry the selected performance, pace and visual treatment into coverage;
neither movement nor stillness is a default.
State enough visible end-state detail to support the next shot's continuity.
Distinguish actor movement from camera movement and maintain screen direction.

## 4. Fit the request without changing its unit of work

Keep editorial shots and generated source segments separate. Preserve the
requested generation count, ratio and continuous take. Native cuts inside one
generation are possible only under the selected runtime capabilities; they
do not authorize additional generations. A continuous scene can change meaning
through blocking and eyelines without cutting.

Choose durations from capabilities verified by the caller at runtime.
Prompt timestamps are approximate; frame-exact cuts, beat hits and freeze
frames belong to post. Plan handles for edits without claiming exact timing.
If the story cannot fit locked runtime or speech, explain the conflict and
offer a smaller story scope or authorized runtime/copy change. Do not silently
drop the ending, speed up dialogue or split into more paid work.

Respect language and exact dialogue. Allow delivery, reaction and final tail
using measured or language-informed timing, not a universal speaking rate.
Separate native dialogue from any requested audio-first or existing soundtrack
route; do not introduce lip-sync production by default.

## 5. Write the prompt and editorial package

Provide complete per-source prose with subject/bindings, action, environment,
visual style, camera/cuts and audio. Carry knowledge-changing behavior, eyelines,
prop states, named light and end states into the prompt, not just the table.
State literal visible actions rather than asking the model to "show a reversal."
Use authorized words with speaker and language; exclude planning terminology
from dialogue unless the actual script calls for it.

Separate request intent: model selection, ratio, runtime, generation count,
allowed cuts, ordered reference roles and audio route. The caller resolves
live-supported parameters. For silent/post-audio work specify
`generate_audio: false` when supported; otherwise flag the authorized
mute/discard route. Never mix generated and supplied speech unintentionally.

Return an edit handoff with source windows, state-matched boundaries, reaction
holds, sound cues, exact post copy, CTA destination and reference provenance.
All added overlays, CTA, graphic logos, captions and UI are deterministic post.
Inspect printed product markings against selected references throughout.
For exact product appearance propose authorized imagery/compositing rather
than claiming references guarantee fidelity. Use actual placement templates;
unknown safe zones remain unverified.

Protect likeness, voice, footage, music and brand rights. A fictional character
is not a real customer and cannot supply actual-use evidence. Do not disguise
privacy-rejected references. Paid-delivery disclosure is the user's separate
decision under applicable policy, not an invented label or automatic approval.

## 6. Repair and check

For a narrow change identify the requested adjustment and smallest affected beat.
Preserve locked goal, facts, logo asset, palette, typography, tone, exact copy,
casting, wardrobe, reveal deadline, ending, count and unrelated shots.
For an explicitly requested creative reset, separate what the user retains
from what they authorize to change. Earlier agent-chosen restraint is not a
user preference; propose a new story when the old premise is the problem.
Do not turn a reset into a tiny gesture repair or silently rewrite historical
selections. A skill-only update does not reopen or regenerate any production.
Classify the requested change as story, performance, treatment, timing or
finishing. For a narrow repair, return only the changed beat/prompt and affected
handoff fields; a warmer reaction does not authorize a new slogan or a full
regeneration. For a creative reset, return the authorized replacement concept
or script within retained constraints, not an unsolicited production restart.
Trace affected continuity, timing and adjacent edit boundaries.
Proposed facts stay hypothetical until accepted; do not rewrite history.

Check observable geography, readable action, information order, product reveal
and continuity. Under the five-element engine, verify each resolved unit has
a supported reversal and an intended before/after character verdict. Under
another selected form, use its criteria instead. Do not call a product reveal
alone a value shift. Check the story against the intended experience and any
selected form, plus brand anchors, claim scope, exact-copy separation and timing.
Do not fail a story merely for lacking conflict, dialogue, action or a twist.
Motion needs playback; sound needs listening. Mark absent media untested.
No craft check predicts virality, conversion or platform approval.

## Requested batches

Plan multiple ads only when requested. Use one compact row per concept:
`product_id / concept_id / requested ad count / story idea /
product role / ending / brand anchors / fact_ids / source count /
unresolved inputs`. Validate totals against the requested ads per product.
Ad count is not source count; unknown source counts remain unresolved until
the caller verifies capabilities and the selected scope.

Keep product identity and claims scoped to each row; share brand anchors only
where supplied as shared. Distinguish new concepts by situation, relationship,
information order or payoff, not product-name swaps. An approved hero with
one-axis variants is a variant-matrix handoff, not permission to invent new
plots or a factorial test. Never multiply unspecified counts, scaffold
projects or submit tasks from this leaf.

## Caller boundary

Honor review_mode independently of approval_mode. Prompt-only work generates
nothing. Preserve the selected exact model and saved defaults. The caller
resolves provider, model, operation, transport and schema revision independently
of capability evidence under
[Seedance execution profiles](../../contracts/seedance-execution-profiles.md).
A family name is not a native Draft guarantee.

Every new generation on the exact legacy native bindings listed in that
contract requires `resolution: "480p"` and boolean `draft: true` in both review modes.
Resolve missing native controls before submission; never drop the flag.
Other models use their verified operation profile: evidenced native controls,
non-promotable ordinary preview controls, or optional evidenced absent preview.
Never invent 480p, Draft or preview controls. Unknown required support remains
unresolved for execution, not a barrier to prompt-only writing.

Native promotion requires verified support and a selected, registered successful
native source with unchanged inputs and current profile evidence. A required
ordinary preview needs a hash-bound selection before a separately registered
final with explicit ordinary-final lineage; it cannot use native task-ID promotion.
Inspect actual finals again. Never silently switch model, transport or preview
route, bypass required previews or expand generation scope. Surface source-count
conflicts before generation. Production checks are caller-owned, as are
rights and disclosure decisions. Return only the needed package or revision.
Optional caller composition may use film-production for execution,
scene-craft for deeper scene audits, seedance-prompt for model grammar,
product-intake and brand-kit for sourced inputs, ad-variant-matrix for hero
variants, and acting, camera or exact-graphics specialists. These are prose
hints only, not dependencies or instructions to load siblings.
