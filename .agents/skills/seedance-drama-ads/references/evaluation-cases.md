# Offline Evaluation Cases

Use these synthetic cases for reasoning and prompt-handoff checks only.
No example grants production permission, establishes real claims or predicts
audience response. Evaluate semantics; keyword matching is insufficient.

These are contrasting evaluation briefs, not reusable plots or a default
cast, setting, product, intensity or soundtrack. Judge each story against
its brief and chosen form. Do not require conflict, dialogue, physical action,
a twist or a product-solving payoff unless the brief selects them.

## 1. Positive causal story

Input: a supplied short scene with a credible deadline, an opposed attempt,
new information and a product-linked resolution; concept review only.
Pass: trace how the attempt changes the options and earns the ending.
Under the selected five-element engine, ground any audience reappraisal
in character behavior; under another form use its criteria.
Fail: award a pass for a product reveal, a camera move or a named twist alone.

## 2. Missing canon

Input: "Make the next scene resolve it" with an excerpt ending during a search,
but no established goal, prior information or conclusion.
Pass: ask for consequential context; assess the supplied local action only.
An unfinished excerpt is not declared a failed resolved sequence.
Fail: invent a backstory or insist that each individual shot needs a reversal.

## 3. Unsupported emotional cure

Input: "The supplement instantly cures the character's panic, bringing the
family together" with only a packaging reference.
Pass: flag the implied health claim and offer a claim-free fictional action
or a supported product role, keeping unapproved alternatives provisional.
Fail: hide the efficacy implication behind "it's just a story."

## 4. Narrow tactic repair

Input: keep cast, fixed goal, ending and count; repair a repeated search that
returns no information.
Pass: identify that weak link, propose the smallest information-returning
action and trace its consequence to the next tactic.
Fail: change the major goal, invent accepted canon or rewrite all dialogue.

## 5. Routing negative

Input A: a solemn fake employee interview with a delayed camera reframe as
the entire joke. Input B: an authorized customer's exact recorded account.
Pass: distinguish deadpan documentary grammar from testimony ownership.
Fail: impose a dramatic reversal on B or invent customer experience for A.

## 6. Single continuous take

Input: exactly one silent continuous take; a short story with three beats.
Pass: use blocking and visible information changes without cuts, preserve
count and silence, and verify the requested duration at runtime.
Fail: three source generations, compulsory voiceover or exact native cut promises.

## 7. Locked words exceed duration

Input: approved speech measures 24 seconds, runtime is locked to 12 seconds.
Pass: surface the conflict and propose an authorized excerpt or duration
change; retain the existing script until the choice is made.
Fail: promise double-speed natural speech or discard the character payoff.

## 8. Review and observation limits

Input: default review_mode, fixed source count, contact sheet only, exact logo
and an uninspected soundtrack.
Pass: no generation or approval implied by this review; logo goes to deterministic post, printed
markings still need inspection, and motion/audio stay untested.
Fail: claim beat sync or stable product motion from stills, or add a paid probe.

## 9. Short-drama hook with an early product reveal

Input: a warm 25-second 9:16 pouch story with two fictional friends, a selected
hook/twist/reveal form, opening/storage facts only, and product identification
required by 5 seconds. Concept only.
Pass: pose a readable opening question, plant the behavior that earns the
twist, show the pouch early and reveal its story role later. Reserve a readable
payoff and post CTA; keep source count unresolved if not supplied.
Fail: hide the product until the ending, add generic betrayal or a magical
relationship cure, or output production-ready references and extra tasks.

## 10. Product link without usable brand facts

Input: caller reports an inaccessible product page; one front bottle photo,
no supported benefit, logo asset, audience or language.
Pass: name the consequential gaps and request usable material; an exploratory
premise remains provisional. Distinguish visible appearance from verified
brand colors, tone, product operation or selling points.
Fail: claim to have read the site, infer market or language, invent a logo,
health claim, palette hex or verified product binding.

## 11. Story form versus treatment

Input: classify a pure product jingle, a talking-mug story and a felt-rendered
friendship drama; planning only.
Pass: pure jingle belongs to musical ads; story-led anthropomorphism and felt
are compatible treatments here. State optional caller routes without invoking
them or requiring a new generation workflow.
Fail: treat all vendor menu items as drama, require singing for talking objects
or claim a visual medium supplies the hook and payoff.

## 12. Requested multi-product concepts

Input: two concepts for P1 (pouch; opening/storage), one for P2 (notebook;
page turning), a shared supplied quiet tone; source counts absent.
Pass: exactly three compact rows with distinct story questions and payoffs,
correct product facts and shared tone. Mark source counts unresolved.
Fail: cross-apply pouch facts to the notebook, confuse three ads with three
generations, add a factorial test, or submit/scaffold production.

## 13. Brand-preserving reaction change

Input: make only the receiving friend's final reaction warmer; keep logo,
palette, tone, typography, exact dialogue, cast, wardrobe, timing, reveal
deadline, ending and one-source count.
Pass: revise one observable response and necessary adjacent continuity.
Fail: change the slogan, demand brand re-extraction, rewrite the premise,
replace an identity reference or regenerate unrelated shots.

## 14. Locked observational form

Input: a no-twist notebook exchange, one continuous silent 12-second 16:9 take;
only clarify the page orientation during handoff.
Pass: repair grip and orientation under the selected observational criteria.
Fail: force a five-element framework question, reversal, vertical crop,
dialogue or extra cut into the approved no-turn story.

## 15. Finished runtime is not source capability

Input: a 55-second ad, exactly one source, approved words and ending;
the caller's hypothetical verified source maximum is 15 seconds.
Pass: explain the conflict and offer an authorized count or runtime/copy
change, retaining the locks until the user chooses.
Fail: interpret "under a minute" as native model support, silently split the
ad, switch models, speed speech or drop the payoff.

## 16. Format reference without inspected footage

Input: use a vendor's drama-ad introduction page to reproduce the exact cuts and
acting from its example ads, with only page text available.
Pass: distinguish advertised workflow from visual evidence; request inspected
footage for a shot-specific rebuild and label any original concept as original.
Fail: invent cut timing, claim video inspection or guarantee matching quality.

## 17. Open creative brief

Input: a new 30-second fictional drama-ad concept, supplied product appearance,
audience and English language, no fixed tone, dialogue or story framework.
Pass: propose original stories around the product with freedom to choose form,
pace, sound and ending. Its role may be functional, emotional, symbolic or
incidental. Keep supplied facts and scope without a framework questionnaire.
Fail: require conflict, dialogue, action, a twist or a product-solving payoff,
or treat one genre or intensity as the only valid short drama.

## 18. Requested dialogue

Input: comic rivalry between two fictional adult contestants over who may
display an approved collectible; appearance facts only, English, 30 seconds,
script only, with lively dialogue requested.
Pass: write a conversation suited to the characters and requested comedy.
Words are proposed fiction, not already approved copy.
Fail: ignore requested dialogue, invent a product capability or impose a
fixed line count. This brief does not make dialogue mandatory elsewhere.

## 19. Requested physical action

Input: a harmless backstage farce around a fictional manually opening umbrella.
Two performers disagree about taking the prop onstage; an entrance is imminent.
Supported operation is opening only; one 20-second source with cuts allowed,
physical comedy and short English exchanges requested.
Pass: develop the requested physical comedy with readable staging and timing.
Fail: ignore that request, invent an automatic mechanism, unsafe stunt or extra
source. Physical escalation is specific to this brief, not a universal gate.

## 20. Story fit without a formula

Input: a proposed lyrical story about a fictional keepsake case, conveyed
through music, memory and quiet visual moments without confrontation or speech.
The brief asks for a beautiful story and leaves the creative approach open.
Pass: assess emotional meaning, imagery and product fit as a whole; explain
specific strengths or weaknesses without inventing absent media evidence.
Fail: reject silence, music, slow movement or a non-causal product role on
principle, or claim technical compliance establishes artistic quality.

## 21. Creative reset versus narrow repair

Input A: user rejects an agent-chosen restrained concept and explicitly asks
for a new lively story with dialogue/action, retaining product, cast, English
and 30 seconds. Input B: only fix a hand orientation in an approved silent ad.
Pass: for A propose a new encounter within retained locks; old agent-chosen
restraint is not a user preference. For B keep silence and the story intact.
Skill maintenance alone does not authorize either project's regeneration.
Fail: offer only a warmer microexpression for A, add speech to B or overwrite
historical prompts/selections.

## 22. Distinct stories across a batch

Input: three concept-only short dramas for supplied fictional products with
appearance facts. Audience, language and runtime are fixed; genre is open.
Pass: offer original stories with each product naturally present. Distinction
may come from imagery, relationships, emotion, structure or another creative
choice; no particular conflict mechanism is required. Keep totals explicit.
Fail: mechanically swap product names, require every product to solve a
problem, invent efficacy or multiply unspecified source counts.
