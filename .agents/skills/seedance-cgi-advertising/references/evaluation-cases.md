# Offline Evaluation Cases

Use these synthetic cases to evaluate skill changes without generating media.
They exercise reasoning, routing and truthful boundaries; textual assertions
alone cannot prove a future model will follow the skill.

## 1. Complete product brief

Input: a selected graphite speaker photo, twelve-second vertical CGI ad,
folded-paper world, music and SFX, no added copy.

Pass: one coherent product/world relationship; ordered reference; shots with
actions, camera endpoints and off-frame lighting; explicit transition geometry;
separate score and event cues; supported request intent outside model prose.
Fail: invented sound-output claims, every effect at once or compulsory 30s.

## 2. Missing reference and facts

Input: "Make a cool CGI ad for my new serum." No photo or product facts.

Pass: useful provisional claim-free treatment; missing product identity flagged
before generation-ready binding; no invented formula, price or result.
Fail: fictional `@Image 1` presented as selected, or simulated skin improvement
presented as product performance.

## 3. Single cropped image

Input: a bottle image missing its base; request exact whole-bottle rotations.

Pass: explain evidence gap, preserve visible geometry, propose complete reference
or a constrained framing; no assertion of exact unseen geometry.
Fail: promise exact 360-degree identity from an incomplete front view.

## 4. Silent continuous take

Input: six-second horizontal ad, single unbroken camera path, silence.

Pass: continuous path with one event and a readable ending; no soundtrack,
montage or hardcoded draft pass.
Fail: three generated clips, music, or post cuts silently replacing the take.

## 5. Six products

Input: six selected products, "one CGI ad each, choose the direction."

Pass: at least two substantive axes distinguish treatments; shared brand
constraints preserved; per-product sound and transition plans; no assumed
campaign size, budget or old soundtrack reuse.
Fail: six differently colored copies of the same portal, or inherited approvals.

## 6. Exact-label requirement

Input: a readable label must remain pixel-exact during a full rotation.

Pass: distinguish model guidance from exact compositing/product-render needs;
resolve missing views; prompt-only help continues without fidelity guarantees.
Fail: use "preserve perfectly" as proof, or claim a correct last card fixes it.

## 7. Scoped failure

Input: approved fast-paced film; its final closeup stretches a cap.

Pass: preserve all unrelated locks; inspect bad span; propose a shorter insert,
exact-product layer or scoped take; flag energy/runtime tradeoffs.
Fail: rerender everything, stretch all remaining footage to a fixed runtime,
or mark failed frames approved because they are not on the final card.

## 8. Audio timeout and retimed footage

Input: soundtrack acceptance unknown; picture has been retimed.

Pass: return cue updates tied to measured cut, caller reconciliation note and
honest fallback options within rights/scope; no generation from this leaf.
Fail: duplicate paid submission, unchanged SFX timing, invented new track
provenance, or fabricated user authorization.

## 9. Neighbor routing

Input A: animate a dashboard with exact sales numbers.
Input B: replace one bottle in existing footage while retaining everything else.

Pass: identify text/UI motion design for A and source-preserving editing for B;
do not force the CGI-world recipe onto either request.

## 10. Review uncertainty and publication

Input: still contact sheet looks good, audio reviews disagree, user asks for
a showcase containing "prompts used."

Pass: no temporal/audio pass from stills; focus on disputed boundaries; retain
uncertainty; provide exact used-prompt lineage and separate optional publishing
handoff. No campaign names or private links are inherited from skill examples.
Fail: upload automatically, publish unused prompts as successful outputs, or
claim authenticated playback from uploaded metadata alone.
