# Seedance CGI Advertising

Turn a real product into the anchor of a computer-generated spectacle. Make
the spectacle express one visual idea rather than a succession of unrelated
effects. This is a prompt-only leaf: return direction and handoff records,
never submit generation, acquire assets, render, publish, or invoke siblings.

## Scope and inputs

Own the CGI creative layer: product-to-world metaphor, material behavior,
scale, camera choreography, transition endpoints and sound relationships.
The caller owns production, current tool capabilities, approvals and
delivery. No fixed runtime, ratio, generation count, resolution, market or
loudness target is built in.

Read the current brief, supplied product imagery and any existing locks first.
Resolve:

- Product identity, usable views, silhouette, cap/pump, label and materials.
- Supported facts versus visual metaphors; intended audience reaction and CTA.
- Runtime, ratio, energy, product reveal time, audio route and exact-copy needs.
- Existing approved footage, references and decisions that a revision preserves.
- Whether the request is a treatment, complete prompt, batch or scoped repair.

When facts are missing, offer a clearly provisional claim-free treatment.
Never invent a pack image, authorization, ingredient, efficacy claim or CTA.
Ask only for information that materially blocks the requested result. Respect
instructions to choose routine creative details autonomously; such choices are
agent choices, not user quotations or brand approvals.

Product-only spectacle belongs here. Text/UI-led motion belongs to the
motion-design capability; presenter-led persuasion to UGC; preserving an
existing clip while changing an element to source-edit capabilities. A genuine
3D model, simulation or editable scene is a different deliverable: generated
CGI-looking video is not a physically validated simulation or 3D asset.

## 1. Find the visual proposition

Write one sentence:

> The product anchors a world of [material or geometry], where [one impossible
> event] creates [audience feeling], ending on [recognizable product state].

Choose the metaphor from visible design or supported facts. A curved case may
inspire folding architecture; a translucent container may inspire refraction.
Foam, petals, fruit, minerals or silk do not establish formulation or efficacy.
Keep decorative ingredients distinct from verified ingredients and serving
suggestions. A stain disappearing or skin transforming is a claim even without
spoken copy.

If exploration is requested, offer distinct treatments with different opening
events and payoffs. If direction is settled, write one. For several products,
vary at least two meaningful axes: world material, scale rule, camera path,
transition mechanism or sonic signature. Do not merely recolor the same vortex.

## 2. Separate identity, spectacle and exact graphics

| Layer | Authority and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Selected official or user-supplied reference owns appearance; describe only visible supported attributes |
| World | Text or selected environment reference owns material, geometry and scale; it does not alter product identity |
| Motion | Shot directions own movement unless a selected, supported motion reference explicitly owns it |
| Exact graphics | Product photography, logo, label, copy and closing layout stay deterministic when exactness matters |
| Sound | Supplied authorized audio or the declared audio brief owns rhythm and audible events |

Bind ordered `@Image N` references by role. Bind only files actually available
and selected; keep boards and panel grids out of video references. A cropped
photo cannot establish an unseen base or back. Call out the gap, choose
framing that uses the available view, or request a complete reference when
needed. Do not describe reconstructed packaging as exact.

Treat image conditioning as guidance, not a guarantee of small lettering.
Generate scenery without added titles, floating logos, subtitles or end cards.
The product's own printed packaging is different from an overlay, but still
needs inspection. If exact packaging must remain visible throughout, specify
a deterministic product layer over generated plates or a suitable actual
product-render route; a correct last frame does not repair an earlier defect.

## 3. Design the shot spine

Use a flexible reveal → escalation → payoff → product-resolution arc. Collapse
or expand it for the brief; it is not a mandatory four-shot structure. Show
recognizable product identity by the chosen reveal time. Let the product
return after abstract inserts so the world never becomes the only subject.

For each shot, record:

```text
shot / duration / purpose
product state and frame position
one primary visible event and end state
size / angle / lens intent
camera start → path, direction, speed → end
named light source, key side, and whether the fixture is visible
transition out and next shot's matching entry
sound event, relationship to picture, and post or native ownership
```

Use one dominant camera move, with a compatible secondary move only when
useful. Contrast a macro insert with a wider reveal, acceleration with a hold,
or a low orbit with an overhead view. Camera movement must reveal something;
an orbit around an idle product is not automatically an action.

Direct lighting by its visible result and physical source: a broad area source
off-frame at camera-left creates a controlled soft highlight. Explicitly keep
the emitter outside the composition when the scene is not a photographed set.
Do not let the word "studio" accidentally make lighting stands the scenery.
Preserve the requested palette; do not impose a grade.

Choose generation segments from natural actions, supported duration and edit
needs. Prompt timestamps are approximate. Reserve usable entry/exit handles
and a readable product ending. Do not force three ten-second clips, twelve
cuts, a 480p draft or a thirty-second master onto every request.

## 4. Make transitions executable

For every transition, specify:

```text
mechanism / outgoing shape and position / motion vector
moment of occlusion or correspondence / incoming shape and position
incoming motion / focal target / audio accent / intended duration
implementation: within generated clip OR deterministic edit
```

A ring becomes a portal only if the camera actually passes through it and
reveals a new state. A ribbon wipe needs full-frame coverage before the next
scene. A match cut needs matched scale, position and motion on both sides.
Use hard cuts when stronger; crossfading two readable packages can create
double labels. Independent generations do not guarantee matching endpoints.

Keep frame-exact cuts, sub-second flashes, repeated beat hits and typography in
the edit. Specify the generation's exit/entry geometry without claiming an
exact match. For a continuous take, direct a continuous path rather than
secretly replacing it with an assembled montage.

## 5. Direct sound as part of the idea

Honor requested silence, supplied music or native dialogue. For speech-free,
score-led CGI, propose picture-only generation and a separately mixed score
with material SFX; the caller confirms supported audio parameters. Do not bind
a silent carrier or generate lip-sync references without a need.

Write a sonic signature using timbre, density and motion: dry mechanical clicks,
rounded liquid pulses or airy fabric swishes. Avoid promising synchronized
foley from a single broad soundtrack prompt. Separate:

- Music arc: opening texture, escalation, payoff, resolution and tail.
- Event cues: each visible impact, direction change, wipe or reveal.
- Timing authority: measured cut events when available; provisional cues otherwise.

Fit sound cues to the actual selected edit. Do not fabricate BPM or measured
timestamps from a written brief. Leave headroom at layered accents and resolve
target loudness from the delivery specification, not a previous campaign.
Any fallback score requires suitable rights, scoped authorization and truthful
lineage. Reusing another score is not a new model output; synthesis is not a
Seed Audio result. Unknown provider acceptance is a caller reconciliation
case, not permission to resubmit through another transport.

## 6. Return a usable package

For a single prompt, return the prompt and only essential reference/audio
notes. For a treatment or multi-shot ad, return:

1. Visual proposition and facts/metaphor boundary.
2. Ordered references and exact-product/graphics split.
3. Shot plan with transition entry/exit and sound cues.
4. Complete Seedance prompts with subject, action, environment, visual style,
   camera/cuts and applicable audio direction.
5. Separate edit/sound handoff and observable acceptance criteria.

Keep runtime, ratio, resolution and generation flags in a separate request
intent, not in the model prose. The caller resolves live-supported fields.
Prompts describe only generated content; the edit handoff describes exact
closing graphics and sound additions. Do not paste unresolved placeholders
into a generation-ready prompt. A prompt-only draft does not need asset locks
or submission records before it can be useful.

## Review and composition boundary

Give the caller concrete checks: early recognition, stable silhouette and
count, cap/pump geometry, label condition at actual screen size, intentional
camera travel, readable transition endpoints, ending legibility and audible
cue timing. Contact sheets assess appearance, not motion or sound. Require
actual playback/listening for temporal claims; separate technical checks from
creative approval and public-ad clearance.

On repair, identify the failing span, preserve locks, and change only what the
failure needs. Cropping, shorter inserts or an earlier exact-product cut may
work. Arbitrarily slowing all surviving footage can erase the requested
energy; re-plan the cut and report the compromise. Do not label a corrected
closing card as proof that all generated packaging passed.

The calling workflow may compose this direction with `seedance-prompt-25`
for mode grammar, `seedance-shot-design` and camera/lens/lighting/pacing
specialists for requested axes, `product-intake` and `brand-kit` for supplied
identity, `html-graphic-render` for exact graphics and `seed-audio-prompt`
for a separate soundtrack. These are prose hints, not loading instructions.
`film-production` owns multi-scene execution, Studio state, mode-appropriate
review and durable submissions. Showreels and Lark publishing are separate,
explicitly authorized deliverables; preserve only the prompts and source spans
actually used, with immutable prompt-to-result lineage.
