# Hook library

Read one entry when a brief asks for a hook, hook alternatives or a hook-only
revision. Each entry is a **creative starting point** for the opening beat of a
mode prompt, not a ranking, an API requirement or a demonstrated result.

## Rules for every hook

- **A hook replaces the opening beat only** (0–2 s, at most 3 s). It never
  changes product facts, locked copy, supported claims, the mode or the CTA.
- **One hook per final prompt.** Alternatives are separate prompts that differ
  in viewer question and opening image, not in wording alone.
- **A hook and a reference-ad recreation are mutually exclusive.** When the
  structure comes from a reference ad (a quick structure clone or a reference
  edit rebuild), the reference's opening is the hook; do not stack a library
  hook on top.
- **The hook must lead into a supported payoff.** The beat after the hook shows
  or says something the product facts support.
- **Spoken hooks are short.** Native speech lands 0.5–1.5 s late (a workspace
  observation, not a platform figure), so put a
  visible action at 0 s and keep the hook line to about five words. On-screen
  hook text is a finishing layer, never prompted into the clip.
- **Testimony is evidence, not texture.** Social-proof and skeptic hooks need a
  real, approved testimonial used within its approved scope, or an obviously
  fictional dramatization that is never framed as a real customer (under the
  FTC Q&A, an obviously fictional dramatization is not a testimonial). Never
  present a synthetic presenter as a real customer, and never invent review
  counts, ratings or quotes.
- **The brief supplies the market.** Entries assume no country, language,
  culture or platform; setting, wardrobe and wording come from the brief.

## Evidence

Verified on 2026-10-17; re-verify before relying on any figure.

- **Official timing anchor:** TikTok's
  [Video Advertising Guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/video-advertising-guide)
  advises hooking viewers in the first 3 s and lists four hook options: "Show
  the product in action right away", "Ask a relatable question", "Drop a bold
  statement" and "Reveal a transformation or surprise visual". Its
  [Creative Codes one-pager](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_One_Pager_CA.pdf)
  (May 2023, Canada edition) stresses the first 6 s for recall and the value
  proposition. Meta and YouTube publish no hook taxonomy.
- **Hook types are practitioner taxonomy (low confidence).** No platform
  publishes which hook type performs best.
- **Hook rate** (3-second views ÷ impressions) has **no official benchmark**;
  vendor figures disagree. Compare hook variants within one account and one
  audience, and never promise a number.
- **Testimonials:** the FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials
  ([16 CFR 465, Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers))
  prohibits fake testimonials, including AI-generated reviews from people who
  do not exist. It has no blanket prohibition on AI avatars, and an obviously
  fictional dramatization is not a testimonial. Other jurisdictions have their
  own rules; check the target market's. Not legal advice; re-verify.

## Stunt hooks

A stunt hook stops the scroll with a physical surprise. Keep the product rigid,
fully in frame and label-side to camera through the action.

### Product Interrupt <!-- id: hook-product-interrupt -->
- **Job:** Break the expected opening with the product physically entering the
  frame mid-action.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, ugc-unboxing, product-review, ugc-virtual-try-on, wild-card
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-1.5 s the presenter looks silently at the
  lens in an eye-level medium shot; her right hand, frame-left, lifts the
  product up into the foreground and stops it level with her chin, label toward
  the lens. At 1.5-2 s she holds it still and starts her first line."
- **Audio:** Diegetic room sound and the soft rustle of the lift; no speech
  until the product stops at about 1.5 s.
- **Risks:** Fast motion blurs or deforms the product; a cut-off sentence reads
  as an error. Keep the move short and the product rigid.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner pattern-interrupt type; no measured advantage.

### Toss and Catch <!-- id: hook-toss-catch -->
- **Job:** Show confident handling and the product's full silhouette in one
  short arc.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-unboxing, product-showcase, wild-card, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-1 s an off-frame hand tosses the product in
  a low arc from frame-left; at 1-2 s the presenter's right hand, frame-left,
  catches it at chest height and turns the label to the lens."
- **Audio:** A soft whoosh and the catch sound; no speech during the arc.
- **Risks:** Mid-air shape and label drift, a second copy appearing, hand
  mirroring. Use a short arc, one product only, and name the catching hand by
  anatomy and frame side. Fragile or liquid-filled products may look careless.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner stunt type; no measured advantage.

## Subtle hooks

A subtle hook earns attention with a question, a viewpoint or a visible result.

### Problem Callout <!-- id: hook-problem-callout -->
- **Job:** Name a specific, recognizable problem the product's supported
  feature addresses.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, product-review, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s a close-up shows the problem as it
  happens (for example a cable slipping off the desk edge); the presenter's
  hand reaches into frame for it."
- **Audio:** The sound of the problem (the slip, the clatter); an optional
  short spoken line naming it.
- **Risks:** Exaggerated harm or fear claims; a problem the product does not
  actually solve. The inferred problem is a hypothesis unless the brief
  supplies it.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner problem-callout type; no measured advantage.

### Point-of-View Interrupt <!-- id: hook-pov-interrupt -->
- **Job:** Put the viewer inside a first-person moment so the product arrives
  as part of their own action.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, ugc-unboxing, ugc-virtual-try-on, wild-card
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s a first-person view looks down at two
  hands on a counter; the right hand, frame-right, picks up the product and
  brings it toward the lens."
- **Audio:** Close diegetic handling sounds; speech, if any, starts after 2 s.
- **Risks:** Hands that do not match a first-person body, extra hands, warped
  perspective. Keep both hands accounted for and the motion slow.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner POV / pattern-interrupt type; no measured advantage.

### Result First <!-- id: hook-result-first -->
- **Job:** Open on the observable end state, then show how it was reached.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, product-showcase, tv-spot, ugc-virtual-try-on, virtual-try-on
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s a locked medium shot holds the finished
  state (the packed bag zipped flat, the assembled stand on the desk); at 2 s
  the shot cuts back to the starting state."
- **Audio:** A short tactile sound on the finished state; no claim in speech.
- **Risks:** Before/after imagery implying unsupported efficacy, especially
  bodily, health or cosmetic outcomes. Show only an operational change the
  facts support; generated imagery is not proof.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner result-first / before-after type; TikTok's Video
  Advertising Guide lists "Reveal a transformation or surprise visual" as a
  hook option. Efficacy claims need substantiation.

### Unboxing Reveal <!-- id: hook-unboxing-reveal -->
- **Job:** Use the moment of opening to create anticipation for the product.
- **Valid modes:** ugc-unboxing, ugc, product-showcase
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s an overhead shot of the closed box on a
  plain table; both hands lift the lid straight up, revealing the product in
  its tray."
- **Audio:** Cardboard friction and the lid release; no speech yet.
- **Risks:** Invented accessories, inserts or printed packaging text. Show only
  the package layers that exist; bind the packaging reference.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner unboxing / reveal type; no measured advantage.

### Open-Loop Question <!-- id: hook-open-loop-question -->
- **Job:** Ask a question the rest of the ad answers with a supported fact or
  demonstration.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, product-review, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s the presenter, in an eye-level medium
  close-up, holds the product beside her face and asks the lens a short
  question; she raises one eyebrow on the last word."
- **Audio:** One spoken question of about five words; room tone.
- **Risks:** A question the ad never answers, or one that implies a result the
  facts do not support. The answer must appear before the CTA.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner question / open-loop type; TikTok's Video
  Advertising Guide lists "Ask a relatable question" as a hook option.

### Contrarian Line <!-- id: hook-contrarian-line -->
- **Job:** Challenge a common assumption, then resolve it with a supported fact.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, product-review, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s the presenter sets down the usual tool
  frame-right and slides the product into its place frame-left, looking at the
  lens as she starts a short line."
- **Audio:** One short spoken line; the set-down sound.
- **Risks:** Disparaging a named or identifiable competitor; an absolute claim
  ("never", "the only") the facts do not support. Keep the "usual way" generic.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner contrarian type; comparison claims need substantiation.

### Skeptic Turned Believer <!-- id: hook-skeptic-turned-believer -->
- **Job:** Open on doubt that the ad then resolves with a supported demonstration.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, product-review
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s the presenter turns the product over in
  both hands in a medium close-up, brows drawn, then looks up at the lens."
- **Audio:** Room tone; a short line only when it is real, approved testimony
  or an obviously fictional dramatization never framed as a real customer.
- **Risks:** A fabricated personal history presented as real. Requires an
  approved testimonial within its scope, or an obviously fictional
  dramatization never framed as a real customer; otherwise stage neutral
  curiosity without a first-person claim.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner type; testimonial rules apply (see Evidence above).

### List Promise <!-- id: hook-list-promise -->
- **Job:** Promise a short countable payoff that structures the rest of the ad.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, product-review, product-showcase
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s the presenter holds up the product in
  her right hand, frame-left, and raises three fingers of her left hand,
  frame-right, toward the lens."
- **Audio:** One short spoken promise naming the count; room tone.
- **Risks:** More listed items than the runtime or the supported facts allow;
  finger counts rendering wrong. Each listed item needs its own beat and a
  supported fact.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner list type; no measured advantage.

### Social Proof <!-- id: hook-social-proof -->
- **Job:** Open with real, approved evidence that other people use or rate the
  product.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, product-review, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s a medium shot of the presenter reacting
  to a phone screen held away from the lens (screen content added in
  finishing), then turning the product toward the camera."
- **Audio:** Room tone; spoken proof only from the supplied, approved source.
- **Risks:** Invented reviews, ratings, counts or quotes; a synthetic presenter
  posed as a real customer. Requires supplied, approved proof with its source;
  the proof itself is added as exact copy in finishing, never generated.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner social-proof type; testimonial rules apply (see
  Evidence above).

### Demo First <!-- id: hook-demo-first -->
- **Job:** Start directly on the product's supported mechanism in use.
- **Valid modes:** ugc, ugc-how-to, product-showcase, product-review, tv-spot
- **Seedance beat (0–2 s):** "At 0-2 s a close-up of the presenter's right
  hand, frame-left, pressing the latch; the lid springs open and stays open."
- **Audio:** The mechanism's own sound; speech starts after the action.
- **Risks:** A mechanism the facts do not describe; occluded hands hiding the
  operation. Keep hands, product and result in one view.
- **Evidence:** Practitioner demo-first type; TikTok guidance lists showing the
  product in use as one hook option.
