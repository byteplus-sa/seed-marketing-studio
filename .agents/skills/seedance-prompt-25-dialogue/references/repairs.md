# Repairs

Read [the entrypoint](../SKILL.md). Choose repairs from the current recording,
approved words and agreed voice, pronunciation and delivery target. A previous
take or language-specific experiment does not establish a universal rule.

## Order of response

1. **Verify.** Listen to the affected phrase in context, using native/tool
   perception or a documented human listening review. Compare a speech-to-text
   transcript against the approved words as supporting evidence; a matching
   transcript cannot certify pronunciation, stress, tone or accent. If listening
   is unavailable, keep the judgment unresolved.
2. **Classify.** Wrong word, mispronounced word, delivery, pacing, speaker or
   alignment.
3. **Choose the smallest repair.**

| Problem | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| One word wrong or mispronounced in an otherwise good take | Retake the clip with one pronunciation cue outside the braces; if the retake changes the performance, splice only the word instead |
| Delivery or intent weak | Revise the delivery direction, not the words |
| Several words wrong across a clip | Retake the clip with the same words; do not re-voice the whole series |
| Line cannot be pronounced after a retake | Offer an alternative wording as a draft for the user to accept |

## Word-scoped splice review

- Replace only the wrong word. Keep the original voice for everything else.
- Listen at normal speed across both boundaries and in the full phrase.
  Compare pronunciation, timbre, pitch contour, cadence, loudness, breath and
  room tone against the agreed target. Inspect visible mouth timing as well.
- Pitch (f0), tempo and waveform measurements can help locate a discontinuity,
  but no universal percentage or splice count determines acceptance. Record
  any project-specific tolerance and its perceptual justification.
- If a word-only splice cannot preserve continuity, explain the tradeoff and
  seek approval for a broader repair rather than silently expanding the edit.
- Never replace a whole line or clip with a re-voiced version unless the user
  asked for it.

## Speech-timing traps

- Check the actual last phoneme and decay against the picture boundary. Add
  room for the ending only when needed and authorized; there is no fixed
  last-second cutoff rule.
- Preserve the source soundtrack and picture. For authorized re-voicing, use
  only a currently supported reference route and keep the result as a separate
  candidate. Do not assume every model accepts audio-bearing source video.

## Record the result

After each take, record the approved text, agreed target, what was heard,
reviewer or listening method, affected interval, cue tried and outcome. Keep
take-specific results in the campaign review record. Reusable guidance should
state its evidence limits, not turn a local result into a language-wide rule.
